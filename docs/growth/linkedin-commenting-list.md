# LinkedIn commenting list

15-20 meaningful comments/week combined across LinkedIn + Reddit (roughly 8-10 on LinkedIn, per `reddit-commenting-playbook.md`'s split). No automation, no canned responses — every comment should be specific to the post it's replying to.

## Where to look

- Posts from the prospect organizations in `backlink-prospects.csv` (categories A and C especially — HR/payroll and personal-finance publishers often post on LinkedIn) discussing compensation, offer letters, payroll, or tax-regime changes.
- Posts using hashtags this account already uses: `#PersonalFinanceIndia`, `#SalaryNegotiation`, `#CTCvsInHand`, `#EPF`, `#CareerAdvice` — search these regularly for active discussions to join, not just to post into.
- HR/recruiter posts specifically discussing "why candidates reject offers" or "compensation transparency" — this account's data is directly relevant evidence, not a tangent.

## Rules

- Add a specific number or calculation when relevant — the same standard as `reddit-commenting-playbook.md`.
- Disclose authorship when citing SalaryExit's own data ("I modeled this at [company]..." reads better than an undisclosed drop-in stat).
- Do not comment on every post from a prospect organization — that reads as farming, not genuine engagement. Comment when there's something specific and additive to say.
- Never comment just to insert a link — a comment with no link and a genuinely useful addition to the conversation is worth more than a comment with a link and nothing else.

## Example

**Post being replied to:** an HR-tech company's post about "why employees are surprised by their first payslip."

**Good comment:**
> This tracks with what we've modeled — at the same CTC, employer-cost structure alone (PF/gratuity/insurance share) can shift monthly in-hand by several thousand rupees. Most onboarding flows don't surface that until the first payslip lands. Happy to share the breakdown if useful for your content.

**Bad comment:**
> Great post! We built a calculator for this: [link]

## Tracking

Same as the Reddit commenting playbook — informal running note, not a formal ledger row per comment. Flag any comment that leads to a DM conversation or content-collaboration interest — that's a lead for `outreach-ledger.csv`, not just a comment.
