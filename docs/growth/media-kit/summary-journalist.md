# Journalist-ready summary: SalaryExit India In-Hand Salary Model 2026

**One-line:** A modeled dataset shows a ₹3L jump in CTC — from ₹12L to ₹15L — adds only about ₹19,150 a month in real take-home pay, because ₹12L is exactly where the new tax regime's Section 87A rebate cliff sits.

**What it is:** SalaryExit India (salaryexit.in), a free Indian salary/tax calculator site, modeled 12 CTC levels (₹5L–₹50L) across 3 employer-cost structures and 2 PF-contribution bases using its own FY 2026-27 tax and PF engine — the same engine behind its public calculators, not a separate one-off analysis.

**The finding that's news-worthy right now:** the "salary cliff" at ₹12L taxable income (Section 87A rebate threshold under the new regime) means candidates negotiating between a ₹12L and ₹15L offer are negotiating over a much smaller real difference than the headline numbers suggest.

**Second finding:** two candidates with the *identical* ₹10L CTC offer can land materially different monthly in-hand pay — purely from how much of that CTC is structured as employer-side cost (PF, gratuity, insurance) vs. fixed cash gross. This is modeled precisely, not anecdotal.

**Data available:** full CSV/JSON download, methodology, and an embeddable table at salaryexit.in/reports/india-in-hand-salary-model-2026 — free to cite or embed with attribution.

**Quote (attributable to Aniket Jadhav, Founder, SalaryExit India):**
> "Most 'salary calculators' just give you a number. We wanted to show *why* two people with the same CTC can take home different amounts — it's not one hidden fee, it's how the whole structure is built."

**What this is not:** not a survey of real employee payslips, not tax filing advice, not a claim about "average Indian salaries" — it's a transparent model, and the report says so explicitly.
