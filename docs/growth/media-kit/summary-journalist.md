# Journalist-ready summary: SalaryExit India In-Hand Salary Model 2026

**One-line:** A modeled dataset finds a precise, ₹70,587-wide band of taxable income (₹12,00,000-₹12,70,587) where the new tax regime's Section 87A marginal relief taxes every additional rupee of income tax (before cess) at exactly 100% — 104% once the 4% cess is included — so a raise in that band produces no net take-home gain.

**What it is:** SalaryExit India (salaryexit.in), a free Indian salary/tax calculator site, modeled 12 CTC levels (₹5L–₹50L) across 3 employer-cost structures and 2 PF-contribution bases using its own FY 2026-27 tax and PF engine — the same engine behind its public calculators, not a separate one-off analysis.

**The finding that's news-worthy right now:** it's not a hard "cliff" — crossing ₹12L taxable income doesn't suddenly cost you tax on your whole income. But for the next ₹70,587 of taxable income, income tax before cess rises at exactly a 100% marginal rate, computed directly from the Section 87A relief formula — and with the 4% health & education cess added on top, the marginal rate on total tax liability in that band is 104%. At the CTC level (13% employer-cost structure), that means moving from ₹12L to ₹15L CTC — a ₹3L/year increase — raises monthly in-hand by only ₹19,150.

**Second finding:** two candidates with the *identical* ₹10L CTC offer can land materially different monthly in-hand pay — purely from how much of that CTC is structured as employer-side cost (PF, gratuity, insurance) vs. fixed cash gross. This is modeled precisely, not anecdotal.

**Data available:** full CSV/JSON download, methodology, and an embeddable table at salaryexit.in/reports/india-in-hand-salary-model-2026 — free to cite or embed with attribution.
